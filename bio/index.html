Research

I have 5+ years of industry ML R&D in digital pathology and multi-omics, from framing clinical and biological questions to building WSI and omics pipelines, designing models, validating across sites and writing papers.

I focus on how tissue morphology in H&E links to molecular expression and drug response, and on checking the basis of each prediction at the level of spatial regions and molecular programs. I control for staining and site confounding and for label noise, withhold predictions on uncertain regions, and examine clinical applicability strictly.

At Deep Bio I worked on breast, prostate, lymph-node and kidney pathology AI and on MFDS regulatory validation. At OMIXAI I work on multi-omics drug-response models for human and canine tumors and on a veterinary-oncology CDSS. I aim to support decisions in precision oncology and target discovery with quantitative models that link pathology, transcriptomics and proteomics.

A reliability map for per-gene multiome RNA velocity parameters in single-cell kinetics

Research summary

We asked whether the per-gene parameters from multiome RNA velocity methods (transcription rate α, degradation rate γ, chromatin-to-transcription lag) are reliable. Five velocity methods were tested on four axes (cross-method reproducibility, an ATAC-shuffle control, replication in five external multiomes, and comparison with measured synthesis and degradation rates). Only α reproduced across methods, and the γ and lag estimates need orthogonal validation.

α reproducibility ρ = 0.88 γ ρ ≈ −0.1 pre-registered 6 / 6 passed

MoSPR: Histology-to-Gene Expression Prediction with Morpho-Spatial Macrostates and Low-Rank Molecular Programs

Research summary

A linear model that groups WSI patches into morphological microstates, finds spatial macrostates from their adjacency, and maps them to gene expression through low-rank molecular programs. It outperformed 14 baselines on three TCGA cancer types and stayed ahead of them when trained on less data. Because the model is linear, each prediction can be decomposed into global and region-specific programs. My part: about half of the experiments, including the comparisons against the 14 baselines, shared with the other co-first author, plus part of the appendix and copy-editing. The idea and main text were led by the first and corresponding author.

Gene-wise PCC BRCA 0.413 · KIRC 0.334 · LUAD 0.358 1st of 15 methods in the paper's benchmark

BIOINFO/GIW ISCB-Asia 2026 Accepted poster Co-first, corresponding & presenting author

Predictability is not substitutability: a cost-of-substitution framework for H&E-based molecular prediction across 5 cancers

Research summary

Seoul · Nov 17–20, 2026. High accuracy does not make an H&E model a substitute for a molecular test. We defined substitution cost as the deviation from a prespecified treatment routing caused by each misclassification, and applied one pre-registered protocol (UNI + CLAM-SB, site-disjoint hold-outs, label-shuffle controls) to five cancers (breast, lung, colorectal, gastric, head and neck). Predictions for lung, gastric and head-and-neck cancer were sealed by commit before any hold-out result existed. Of the 10 sealed endpoints, 3 were positive controls and 7 were candidates: 1 confirmed (HPV status in head and neck, AUROC 0.959) and 6 undecided, each with fewer than the 25 hold-out positives the protocol required. Lung histological subtype, a control, scored highest (AUROC 0.939) but was excluded after a site-confounding audit. Breast and colorectal endpoints were reported but not counted. With Pseudo Lab. My part: designed the evaluation protocol, re-ran and finalized the analysis (including the endometrial extension), and wrote the abstract and manuscript drafts.

Confirmed HNSC HPV · AUROC 0.959 Excluded lung subtype · V(site, label) = 1.0
